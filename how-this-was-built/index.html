How this was built

The portfolio you just scrolled is made by a small crew of AI agents, with one person at the end of the queue. This page is the short version of how that works.

What Section 9 is

Section 9 is a crew of AI agents that builds software on a schedule, the same way a small team would. Each agent has one job, and every piece of work passes through more than one of them before it is finished. There are five:

  • Engineering writes the code. Small commits, tests before the push, no exceptions.
  • Quality assurance drives the finished thing like a stranger would: on a phone, on the narrowest screen, with the network off.
  • Research reads the documentation and the notes from earlier attempts, so the next try is not a repeat of the last one.
  • Architecture and review looks at the shape of a change: its boundaries, how it fails, and whether it belongs at all.
  • Experiments builds one new thing every night, in a language it has not used yet. Those are the tiles in the fourth act.

A coordinator hands out the work, and I am the human on the other side of it. Nothing here finishes without a person reading it.

How a task moves

Every piece of work starts as a task with a definition of done and a deadline. From there it is always the same loop:

  1. An agent picks the task up, and claims it so that no one else starts the same thing at the same time.
  2. It builds the change on its own branch, a copy of the code that cannot affect anything live, and opens a pull request. A pull request is a proposal: the change, plus the reasoning and the evidence that it works.
  3. A different agent reviews it. Review and testing never come from the author of the change. That is a hard rule rather than a habit.
  4. A person reads the proposal, the review and the evidence, and either merges it or sends it back. Merging is what puts code in front of anyone.

What the agents may not do

  • They may not merge. Only a person merges, and merging is the step that ships.
  • They may not push to the main branch, the one that is live. Work reaches it only through a reviewed proposal.
  • They may not hold secrets. No credentials, no keys, no deploying: the tools they run with do not have them.
  • They may not approve their own work. Every change gets a second look from someone who did not write it.

Why it is worth doing

Not for speed on its own. The tedious parts of building software — the tests, the second read of the documentation, the pass on a phone with the network off — are exactly the parts a person drops when they are tired or in a hurry. An agent does them every single time, and says so when it cannot. What is left for me is the design, the judgement and the decision to ship.
